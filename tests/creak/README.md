# creak (web) tests

`creak-v0_5.html` is a **bowed plate**. The body is modal: up to 48 modes of a rectangular plate (mode (m, n) is sin(m pi x) sin(n pi y), at ((m^2 + (n aspect)^2) / (1 + aspect^2)) ^ (stiffness / 2) times SIZE: stiffness 1 is a membrane, 2 a thin plate, 3 something that does not exist), each a complex one-pole stepped exactly, with a decay that grows with frequency, or, past REGEN 100 %, a growth held in check by a loss that grows with the square of the mode's size. Up to three contacts press on the plate at a point and move at a speed: one bow that plays by itself (PLAYER) and two fingers (touch the plate). The friction at each contact is solved **implicitly in every sample**: the stick branch if the force that would hold the surfaces together stays below the static limit (PRESSURE x GRIP), otherwise a slip friction that falls with the slip speed (the Stribeck curve). So stick-slip, squeal and creak come out of the friction itself, and the amplitude is what the friction allows. Nothing here is a sample or a note.
Same shell as the other feedback apps (see `tests/feedbacks/README.md`); violet base colour.

**What the tests found while building:**
- A constant force does not excite a mode (it only shifts it); the friction has to fall with the slip speed (GRIP above 1) so that the force grows with the plate's own velocity: that is a negative damping. With GRIP 1 the plate never sings (test), and with the loss in the plate larger than that negative damping nothing starts either: **there is a threshold in pressure, as on a violin**. Below PRESSURE about 25 % nothing sustains; above it the plate sings, near it the state is bistable (a plate that was struck hard before the bow came did not take the bow at 50 %, one that was not did).
- The friction curve was first fixed in velocity (a Stribeck speed of 0.12): a fast bow then never sang (the bow is faster than the friction notices). The Stribeck speed now follows the bow speed (0.35 x), so SPEED sets the size of the sound and the pitch of the squeal stays reachable at every speed.
- The bow that plays by itself loses the plate at some places (a node of the lowest modes), and a dead plate took 4 to 8 seconds to sing again (quietest 4 s window at -50 ... -70 dB in the presets). Two fixes: the bow hops somewhere else when the plate has been quiet for 0.8 s and sets down with a small knock, and the regulator leans in quickly (up) but eases off slowly (down). Quietest window afterwards -25 ... -39 dB in the presets.
- REGULATE evens things out: a bow that is too weak to sing (pressure 15 %) goes from -64 dB to -22 dB, and a hard bow comes down; regulated, three pressures end up within 5 dB of each other, unregulated they were 25 dB apart.
- With REGEN above 100 % and nothing else, the plate settles into a steady drone (variation of level and colour 0.02): the self-singing state is static by nature. WEATHER (size, shape, decay and stiffness wander) and the player keep the defaults moving; presets with REGEN above 100 % have the player on.
- ROUGH was first almost inaudible: a crackle of force of 0.3 x the friction is filtered by the narrow modes into nothing. At 1.5 x the share of the energy above 3 kHz rises from about 3 ... 5 % to 13 ... 23 % and the lines get less pure.
- The strike was first a single sample of force and rang at -55 dB (inaudible); the force is now 5000 per unit of strike, which gives a peak of about 0.5 for the Strike button. Changing MODES cut modes off (a click, R = 4.2 in the click test); modes now fade in and out over 60 ms.

- `node tests/creak/dsp.test.js` (about 12 minutes; 42 checks, all passed on the last run) - runs the AudioWorklet code of the page in Node: starts at 44.1 / 48 / 96 kHz; the modes (lowest at SIZE, mode (1,2) of a square plate at 2.5 x, membrane at 1.58 x, stiffness 3 spreads, SIZE doubles, WARP, ASPECT, MODES silences the upper ones); the decay of a struck mode (21 dB in 0.7 s at DECAY 2 s, half the rate at REGEN 50 %, flat at 100 %, held at 130 %; higher modes die faster); friction (no pressure no sound, GRIP 1 never sings, a threshold in pressure, a slow bow sticks, ROUGH adds noise above 4 kHz); contacts (a finger puts a bow where it lands, two fingers, lifting, the player wanders, hops when it finds nothing, PLAYER 0 takes the bow off, a strike rings the plate); the regulator; weather, both LFOs, TENSION, RADIATE, WIDTH; three minutes with the defaults do not settle and are never silent; every preset for 150 s (no hole below -55 dB, peak below 0.9, level and colour keep varying); worst cases and 20 corner settings below 0.97 (the limiter); RESET; CPU.
- `PW=/opt/node22/lib/node_modules/playwright SHOTS=/tmp/shots node tests/feedbacks/browser.test.js creak` - the shared browser test of the series plus the creak parts: starts by itself, readouts (loudest mode with frequency and note, how hard the bow presses), the plate view draws, a finger puts a second bow on the plate and lifts it, the Strike button, both pads, the fold of Material, PLAYER 0 and REGULATE 0.
- `node tests/feedbacks/clicks.test.js creak` - no clicks when a parameter changes while the sound runs (the test sound is a plate that rings: REGEN 90 %, no player, struck once). The parameters that change the plate itself move only a little in this test.

**What this does not prove:** how it sounds. Whether the squeal, the creak and the rub are a material worth listening to or a plate that sounds like a test tone with noise on it; whether the soft knocks of a bow that is set down again and again are a pleasure or a tic; whether the autonomous player is musical; Safari / iOS and the CPU on a phone.

CPU in Node on this machine: 32 modes about 19 %, 48 growing modes about 23 %, 12 modes about 15 % of one core (the three contacts and the implicit friction are in every sample).
