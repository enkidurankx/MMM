chua

A chaotic oscillator after Leon Chua's circuit: three coupled equations that generate their own signal. No input, no clock, no microphone, no feedback delay. Silent until you start it. Alpha and Beta move it between a steady tone, a wandering one and chaos; Kick shakes it, Reset starts it again from rest.

Lyapunov exponent–
Pitch (about)–

The exponent tells how fast two almost identical states drift apart, per unit of circuit time. Above 0 it is chaos, around 0 a repeating orbit, below 0 the state settles and the sound dies out (a kick starts it again). Some settings have two stable orbits, a small chaotic one and a large regular one; which you get depends on where the state is. If it sticks in the wrong one, press Reset (a preset does that for you).

2Alpha first capacitor

With Beta at 28, Alpha from about 14 to 18 gives chaos (15.6 is the double scroll; 14 to 15 is a single scroll). Below 14 the orbit is regular, near 19 and above a large, loud one.

LFO 1 → alpha  

Depth 100 % = alpha plus and minus 3. Set it and the circuit wanders in and out of chaos by itself.

3Beta inductor

Beta sets the slow third variable. Near 28 the orbit is chaotic, well above or below it settles into a regular cycle.

LFO 2 → beta  

Depth 100 % = beta plus and minus 10.

4Diode the nonlinearity

Shifts the working point of the Chua diode. At 0 the circuit is symmetric and the state jumps between two scrolls. Away from 0 one scroll wins: single-scroll chaos or a lopsided orbit.

5Time circuit time per second

Rate only changes how fast the same equations run, not what they do: the shape of the orbit stays, the pitch follows. Low rates are a slow drift of the whole state, high ones a pitched, buzzing tone.

6Output

X / Y sends the first variable left and the second right: two views of the same orbit, so the stereo image is the orbit itself. Width 0 folds that to mono. The mean of each variable is removed, and a soft saturation keeps the loud orbits inside the range.
